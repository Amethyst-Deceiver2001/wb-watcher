How I Checked This

Every Wildberries product link looks like this: wildberries.ru/catalog/…/detail.aspx. I started by scanning Telegram channels that run fundraisers for specific military units with links to Wildberries products, then began searching by keyword ("body armor," "plate carrier," "night-vision device," etc.). A Python script (see the repository) then scraped each listing's product name, description, price/stock history, customer reviews, and the seller's legal entity — INN, sole-proprietor registration number, region of registration. Any visitor to the site sees the same data.

Every product is tagged along two independent axes. The first is functional purpose (body armor, first-aid kit, FPV drone, power bank, and so on). The second is military status: "unambiguously military" — items with no civilian use whatsoever in principle (a signal jammer, a VOG grenade tailfin, a BR5-class armor plate); "dual-use, elevated demand" — items sold to civilians too, but bought up mainly by the front (sleeping bags rated to −30°C, combat boots, radios); and everything else. The tagging is built on a dictionary of regular expressions, so it makes mistakes on edge cases. For example, a batch of souvenir pins for the "Krasukha-4" electronic-warfare complex — a metal token unrelated to the actual jamming station — got tagged "unambiguously military" under the first version of the rules purely because it repeated the real equipment's name verbatim; the same kind of mistake also happened in reverse — a "plastic mock armor plate, airsoft plate" was counted as a real BR-class armor plate for a while, simply because the word "armor plate" appeared in the title. Both errors have been fixed.

And of those, 3,910 — about 89% — ship to the buyer from the marketplace's own warehouses. So Wildberries isn't just allowing goods banned by its own contract into the catalog — it's servicing them with its own logistics.

How I counted this, and what this count does not claim. I applied clause 39 of the seller agreement to a given listing only where the product is both, by functional category — body armor, helmet, uniform clothing — and by military status unambiguously "unambiguously military," with no civilian use. Camouflage clothing marketed "for hunting," tactical accessories, and merch didn't make it into this count, even though the wording "goods of a military assortment" may well cover them too.

Who Most Often Sells Military Goods on Wildberries

"Seller" in this piece sounds like a single category, but it covers at least two very different types of participants.

Chinese cross-border resellers. 430 of 802 (54%) legal entities currently trading at least one "unambiguously military" listing — more than half the entire group — are registered in China (a Chinese Unified Social Credit Code instead of a Russian INN). They're simply invisible in volume rankings: 72% of them (311 of 430) hold one or two "unambiguously military" items in a broad, mixed catalog (FPV components, power banks, thermal-imaging optics), so by volume per seller they lose out to Russian monobrand specialists, but by number of distinct legal entities, they form the single largest group of sellers in the whole sample. A single Chinese legal entity typically operates through several different Wildberries storefronts at once — that's not a naming coincidence, it's the same INN under different signs. For example, the company Quanzhou Yier Shunxing Trading Co., Ltd. (code 91350425MA338QHQ0A) trades through 2 storefronts at once ("CYuniversal," "CY2universal") — 137 items, 65 of them "unambiguously military." The company Quanzhou Luojiang Paileihuo Trading Co., Ltd. (code 91350504MAERNYQU64) — through 5 storefronts ("MagnitSC112," "ElstarSC16," "SashinSC115," "AvroraSC14," "BystryySC19") — 160 listings, 69 "unambiguously military."

The single widest catalog in the whole database doesn't belong to an individual seller at all — it runs under the name "Finds from China" (internal supplier IDs 250132124 and 250149024 — 637 listings across 11 categories, 239 of them "unambiguously military"), but this isn't one seller's storefront, it's the platform's own curated selection. Wildberries integrated AliExpress product listings directly into it, keeping the original descriptions, photos, and accumulated reviews. That's why Wildberries' own supplier-by-id lookup publishes no INN, no legal name, no state registration number for these listings — there's simply no single identifiable seller behind the listing to name (for comparison, the exact same query for any of the Chinese sellers named above returns a full legal name and code).

Monobrand military specialists. At the other pole are sellers whose catalog is almost entirely "unambiguously military" items under one brand: sole proprietor Voeikov A.I. "Everything for the SVO"

After Ukrainian drone strikes on Wildberries' warehouses began on July 18, 2026, and continuing to this day, many news outlets reported that the platform removed the "Everything for the SVO" section from its site navigation. That's only half true: in Wildberries' public category tree (the file main-menu-ru-ru-v3.json), there is not, and never was, a section "for the SVO." But it's important to understand what this page actually was to begin with: not a standalone catalog section in the category tree, but a pre-built search query (a "preset") that Wildberries had served for years at a short tag URL. Removing such a page from the menu is not the same as removing it from the catalog — the pages themselves never went anywhere.

What this "preset" actually is, technically, is visible in the service file the platform itself returns before rendering the page:

"url": "/catalog/tags/svo-tovary", "name": "Svo tovary", "searchQuery": "svo tovary", "link": "/catalog/dom-i-dacha"RU«url»: «/catalog/tags/svo-tovary», «name»: «Сво товары», «searchQuery»: «сво товары», «link»: «/catalog/dom-i-dacha»

The name field is just searchQuery with its first letter capitalized, and link (for another tag we found, "kit for the svo," it's literally "/catalog/sport") points to an arbitrary, non-military parent category. In other words, this isn't a hand-built catalog section, it's a templated page the system builds around a literal search query — the very same one real buyers typed into the search bar. Technically, it's the same internal search (u-search/exactmatch, tagged catalog_type: "preset") that serves any ordinary query.

On July 29, 2026 — after the stories about the "Everything for the SVO" section being removed had already been published — I opened a similar page, wildberries.ru/catalog/tags/svo-tovary, checked it, and logged which query hit the platform's server. It carries a relevance score of rs: 100 — an exact match. At the time of checking, this catalog held 9,995 products, including "Body Armor 8301, Tactical Military Plate Carrier, Multicam," an odor neutralizer "for the army, the SVO, Rosgvardiya" from the brand Helmetex, and a tactical first-aid kit "for the SVO."

But if not in a special "for the SVO" section, where in Wildberries' actual, human-built catalog does all this product actually live? I matched the subject_id of each of the 7,666 "unambiguously military" listings against the platform's full category tree (109 root sections, the file subject-base.json, which the platform itself serves from its own CDN). The result held for 100% of listings — and not one landed in a section with a military-sounding name, because no such section exists on Wildberries: 48% sit in "Sporting Goods," another 18% in "Electronics Accessories".

Combat FPV drone components — batteries, frames, antennas — sit in "Electronics Accessories," next to phone cases. Combat helmets share the "Sports Accessories" section (sports helmets) with "Workwear & PPE" (protective hard hats) — but never get a section of their own.

Sometimes this gets absurd: a payload-release mechanism for a combat FPV drone (nm 983152301, "Universal Cargo-Release System for Drones") is filed under the subsection "Sorters" in the section "For Toddlers." Motors and frames for combat drones sit in "Remote-Control Toys," one of them in "Doll Accessories."

In other words, 7,666 doesn't mean "there are 7,666 different military-purpose products on Wildberries" — it's only what I was able to find through a surface-level search.
